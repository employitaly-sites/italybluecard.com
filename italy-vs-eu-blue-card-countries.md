# Italy vs Other EU Blue Card Countries

> Italy compared with Germany and the Netherlands for Blue Card holders: salary thresholds, permanent residence, notice, leave, sick pay, contributions, 13th month, TFR and non-compete.


<section> <span class="eyebrow"><a href="eu-blue-card-italy.html">EU Blue Card</a></span> <h1 id="italy-germany-or-the-netherlands">Italy, Germany or the Netherlands?</h1> <p class="lede">The Blue Card is an EU scheme, but each country sets its own threshold and its own employment rules. Italy's threshold is much lower, which opens the route to more roles and earlier-career profiles. Here is how the three compare.</p> </section>  <section> <h2 id="the-employment-contract">The employment contract</h2>  <p class="prose"><small>Sources, read 4 Nov 2026. General orientation, not legal advice.</small></p> </section> <section class="prose"> <h2 id="what-this-means-for-a-candidate">What this means for a candidate</h2> <p>A role paid above the Italian threshold but below €45,934.20 a year can qualify for a Blue Card in Italy while falling short of both German thresholds, and of the Dutch one. Living costs outside the biggest cities are lower too. In exchange, Italian employment is more structured: a CCNL sets the minimums, and pay is spread over 13 or 14 installments with TFR on top.</p> <h2 id="what-this-means-for-an-employer-abroad">What this means for an employer abroad</h2> <p>Budget the full Italian employment cost, not just the salary: contributions, 13th month and TFR.
