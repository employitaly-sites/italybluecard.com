# Losing Your Job on an Italian Blue Card: What Happens Next

> Dismissal, resignation or the end of a contract: what you receive, how long you can stay, how to change employer, and what the Blue Card allows you to do.

Nobody moves to a new country expecting the job to end. But companies change plans, projects stop, managers move on. If you hold an Italian Blue Card, the law gives you more room than many people think. Here is what happens and what to do.

## If the company ends your employment

Italian employment is not at-will. After probation, dismissal needs a valid reason: just cause, or a justified objective or subjective reason. The CCNL notice period applies, or pay in lieu of notice. Unfair dismissal can lead to compensation or, in some cases, reinstatement.

In your final payslip you receive:

- salary for the days worked and the notice period;
- the accrued share of the 13th and 14th month;
- unused leave;
- the TFR severance accrued during your employment, unless it went monthly into a pension fund.

If you are dismissed you may be entitled to unemployment benefit (NASpI) under the usual rules.

## If you resign

You give the notice set by your CCNL. You receive the same final items except, of course, the notice pay if you do not work the notice. Resignations in Italy are filed online; we help you do it.

## Your permit after the job ends

Article 27-quater gives Blue Card holders specific rights:

- you may **look for and take up another job while unemployed** (paragraph 13-bis);
- in the **first twelve months**, a new job must still match the conditions of the card, and a change of employer requires the **prior approval of the local labour office**, which counts as given if it does not reply within **15 days** (paragraph 13);
- after twelve months, you have equal access to the labour market.

How long you can remain unemployed before the permit is at risk depends on the rules for your permit; we check your case at the time.

## Changing employer

If you find a new job, the new employer must meet the same Blue Card conditions: salary, qualification, contract length. In the first year the labour office approval applies. If the new company has no Italian entity either, we can employ you on its behalf with a new contract.

If your original employer abroad simply wants to stop, but another company is interested in you, tell us early. A seamless switch, where the new contract starts the day after the old one ends, is the best outcome for your permit and your income.

## Self-employment

The Blue Card allows self-employment alongside the qualified job. It is not a replacement for it. If you plan to become a freelancer full time, that is a different permit, and needs advice.

## What to do in the first week

1. Ask for the termination in writing and check the dates.
2. Ask us for your final payslip estimate.
3. Check your permit expiry date.
4. Update your CV and contact companies. Tell them you hold a Blue Card.
5. If dismissed, check NASpI eligibility.
6. If your family is with you, check how their permits depend on yours.

## For the company abroad

If you employ someone in Italy through us and need to end the arrangement, talk to us before telling the employee. The procedure and notice must be right, and a respectful process matters even more when someone has moved their life for the job. We handle the paperwork and explain the cost in advance.

## In short

You are not left without rights. Notice, final pay, TFR, possibly NASpI, and a Blue Card that lets you look for the next job. [Contact us](/contact.html) as soon as you know.

## You might also ask

**Do I have to leave Italy immediately?** No. Ask us what applies to your permit, and use the time to look for a new role.

**Can I get my TFR if it went into a pension fund?** Fund rules decide when and how you can withdraw. It is not paid out by the employer in that case.

**Does unemployment affect my family's permits?** Family permits depend on your status. Keep us informed so we can check.

**Can I claim NASpI if I resign?** As a rule, no, except for resignation for just cause. Check the conditions before resigning.

**What if my dismissal seems unfair?** Italian law lets you challenge it within short deadlines. Speak to a labour lawyer or a union quickly; do not wait until your final payslip.
