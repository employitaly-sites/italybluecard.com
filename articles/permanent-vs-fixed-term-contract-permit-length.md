# Permanent or Fixed-Term Contract: What It Changes for Your Italian Permit

> A permanent contract gives a two-year Blue Card; a fixed-term one gives the contract length plus three months. How contract type affects permit, renewal and planning.

When a company abroad hires you through an Employer of Record, one of the first choices is the contract type: permanent (*a tempo indeterminato*) or fixed-term (*a tempo determinato*). In Italy both are common. For a sponsored employee, the choice also shapes the permit.

## The rule for the Blue Card

Article 27-quater, paragraph 11 is clear:

- with a **permanent contract**, the Blue Card residence permit lasts **two years**;
- with any **other contract**, it lasts the **length of the employment relationship plus three months**.

And paragraph 5 sets a floor: the contract or binding offer must last **at least six months**.

So a twelve-month fixed-term contract gives a permit of about fifteen months, and a renewal tied to a contract extension. A permanent contract gives two years, renewable while the job continues.

## Why companies choose fixed-term

Companies abroad sometimes prefer fixed-term because they are testing a market or a project, or because they are used to at-will employment and see a fixed end date as a safety valve. In Italy, fixed-term contracts have legal limits on total duration and on renewals.

From the permit side, a fixed-term contract means:

- shorter permit, earlier renewal;
- a renewal that depends on the contract being extended or converted;
- less certainty for a lease, a loan or a family reunification file.

## Why we usually suggest permanent

A permanent Italian contract is not a life sentence. It includes a probation period, up to six months depending on the CCNL, during which either side can end it without notice. After probation, ending it needs a valid reason and the CCNL notice period, like any employment in Italy.

In exchange, the employee gets a two-year permit, a stronger position with landlords, and a simpler family file. For a company that is serious about the person, it is usually the better choice.

## Part-time

Part-time contracts are possible and standard in Italy. For the Blue Card, what counts is the salary actually paid, so a part-time contract must still reach the threshold on its own. Few qualified roles do; plan for full time.

## Converting later

A fixed-term contract can be converted into a permanent one. The permit is then renewed on the new terms. If you plan to convert, do it before the permit renewal so the renewal can use the permanent contract.

## What happens at the end of a fixed-term contract

If the contract ends and is not renewed, you receive your final pay, TFR and unused leave. Your permit lasts three months beyond the contract. As a Blue Card holder you may look for another job during unemployment. Talk to us well before the end date: a new employer, or the same company through us on a new contract, needs time to arrange.

## A short comparison

| | Permanent | Fixed-term |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Card permit | 2 years | Contract length + 3 months |
| Minimum length for Blue Card | - | 6 months |
| Ends with | Notice and a valid reason, or resignation | The end date, or earlier for just cause |
| Landlords and banks | Strong | Weaker |
| Family file | Simpler | Tied to contract length |

## Our advice

If the company employing you expects the role to last, choose permanent with a well-used probation period. If it genuinely is a project, choose a fixed term long enough to be worth the move, and agree in advance what happens at the end. ## Doubts we clear up

**Can a fixed-term contract be renewed indefinitely?** No. Italian law limits duration and renewals of fixed-term contracts. We check the plan against the limits.

**Does a permanent contract cost the employer more?** The monthly cost is the same. The difference is in how and when the relationship can end.

**Which is better for a mortgage?** Banks strongly prefer permanent contracts that have passed probation.

**Can I resign from a permanent contract?** Yes, at any time, giving the notice set by your CCNL. A permanent contract binds the employer far more than the employee.

**Does the contract type change my salary or benefits?** No. Pay, leave, 13th month and TFR are the same; fixed-term employees have the same rights for the duration of the contract.

**What if the company is unsure about Italy long term?** A permanent contract with an EOR is still easy to end correctly if plans change, with notice and a valid reason. The company does not take on an entity it may later need to close.
